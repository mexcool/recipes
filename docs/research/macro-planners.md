# Macro / portion-target meal planners: survey and fit against the Sanremo grammature-diet brief

Date: 2026-11-04. Scope: apps that generate meal plans from calorie / macro / portion targets, judged against the brief (fixed-gram Italian "schema a grammature" per meal, vegetables unlimited, variety, two-person household with one constrained member, one weekly shopping list, cost comparison at Carrefour / Coop / Pam in Sanremo).

## 0. Method and evidence quality

- Pages could NOT be opened in this session: the egress proxy answered 403 to every CONNECT (vendor sites, apps.apple.com, play.google.com all failed). Every fact below comes from search-engine snippets of official help centres, app-store listings, vendor pages and third-party reviews.
- Tags: **V** = verified against official help-centre / app-store / vendor text, or at least two independent sources agree. **I** = inference, single secondary source, or competitor blog (nutrola.app, nutriscan.app, promealplan.com, mealthinker.com and similar all sell a rival app).
- Prices are regional and inconsistent between sources (often two different figures for the same app). Treat every price as +/-30% and re-check in the Italian App Store / Play Store before quoting.

## 1. Market status changes (V)

- **PlateJoy shut down on 1 July 2025.** Official help-centre notice dated 8 May 2025; recipes moved to RVO Health's Wellos app; left Google Play in March 2025 (secondary).
- **Mealime shut down on 21 October 2026.** Owner Albertsons folded it into "Meals Hub" inside its US grocery apps (Safeway, Vons, Jewel-Osco); no export, user data deleted; announced via v4.21.35 release notes on 2 Sept 2026.
- Ollie (AI meal planner) announced shutdown for 6 Nov 2026 (single source, I).
- Takeaway: the "household meal planner + grocery list" category is consolidating into grocers and health platforms. Independent survivors are the macro-driven generators (Eat This Much, Prospre, Strongr Fastr, MealPrepPro, Fitia) and the trackers with template plans (Yazio, Lifesum).

## 2. App-by-app

### 2.1 Eat This Much (ETM) - closest existing analogue of the whole loop
- **Targets:** daily kcal + macros, auto from profile or set manually; different targets per weekday (V, help centre). Meals are "Meal Types" (Breakfast, Lunch, ...) with their own settings, shared by all meals of that type (V). A 2026 hands-on review says per-meal nutrition targets and per-day macro splits exist (I); official copy: "every meal is sized to land you on your targets by bedtime" (V).
- **Auto-scaling:** yes, the generator scales food/recipe portions to hit the targets; reviewers call it "adjustable portion sizes to meet exact macro requirements" (V).
- **Variety controls (V, help centre):** Recurring foods (never / sometimes / often / always; "only use recurring" restricts a meal to your own list, docs suggest 15-20 foods to keep variety); "How many recipes to prepare" per meal (1 = single dish, >=2 adds sides); "Generator focus = groceries" reuses ingredients across the week to shorten the list; "Leftovers patterns" (pick which meal type creates leftovers and which consume them; Premium).
- **Main complaints (I, several reviews):** repetition by week 3-4 even with variety maxed; bland/simple meals; database of ~5,000 curated recipes, US-centric foods; English only, no localisation roadmap; oversized grocery lists; regeneration bugs.
- **Grocery list:** weekly, automatic, Premium; Instacart (US only) (V).
- **Household (V, official tutorial #10 + help centre):** "people eating" per meal multiplies quantities for shopping. Two couple methods: (a) set planner targets to the lower person's targets and set every meal to 2 people; (b) sum both people's calories and split meals by each person's share. No per-person targets on a shared dish (I).
- **Price / platform:** free tier = one day at a time with ads; Premium ~US$5/mo billed annually (~$60/yr) or ~$14.99/mo; Italian source quotes EUR 8.99/mo or EUR 47.88/yr. Web + iOS + Android. iOS 4.7 (22k), Android 4.6-4.7 (10k).
- **Fit:** right mental model (fixed targets -> generated varied week -> one list), wrong target semantics (kcal/macros instead of "80 g pasta"), no Italian localisation or stores, household via hacks.

### 2.2 Macrostax
- **Targets:** daily AND per-meal macro grams (C/P/F); the app's signature is showing a macro budget for each meal. Early versions did not allow custom targets (FeastGood 30-day review: "no option to customize your own calories and macros"); current Play listing says you can "customize your own macros" (V/I).
- **Generation:** "Automagic Meal Plans" (2025-26): one tap, daily plan with portions customised to hit macros, meals swappable; grocery list from the plan (V vendor/store). Macrostax tip pages teach "fill remaining macros from the carb list, then the protein list", i.e. portion lists by macro role (V).
- **Complaints (V store reviews):** many recipes lack weight (gram) measurements; grocery list changes when you navigate away; originally "no set meal plans"; small user base (iOS 4.1 star, 142 ratings).
- **Household:** none. **Price:** US$29.99/mo or $179.88/yr (App Store); Play listing shows $19.99/mo, $119.88/yr (conflicting). iOS/Android.
- **Fit:** per-meal budgets and macro-role food lists are conceptually close to a grammature diet, but it is US-centric, expensive and weakly reviewed.

### 2.3 Strongr Fastr
- Web-first planner + workout plans. Daily kcal/macros auto or manual (advanced settings); builds a week from "thousands of meals and recipes"; swap any meal for one that meets your macros; scale servings; grocery list sorted by aisle, checkable; vendor claims small lists by ingredient reuse (V).
- **Complaints (V store reviews):** "bizarre portion sizes and meal combinations to meet macros"; plan degenerates into the same meals every day after about a month; ignores avoid-list; poor onboarding; auto-renew by default.
- **Household:** nothing found beyond "scale servings". **Price:** free tier; Pro $9.99/mo, $49.99/yr, plus 3-/6-month options at $52.99 / $59.99 / $79.99 (App Store). A "lifetime one-time" claim on a third-party site looks wrong. v2.0 redesign Dec 2025 (I). iOS 4.7 (1.8k), Android 4.7 (2k).

### 2.4 Prospre
- **Targets:** any kcal + P/F/C in grams, optionally as ranges; plans land within ~5 g per macro (user report) (V/I). Swap meals or regenerate days; "Fit Into Plan" inserts a treat and rebalances the rest of the day; Recurring Meals and Repeated Day Pattern (Pro); custom recipes (V).
- **Grocery list:** pick a date range, get ingredient amounts; print; Amazon Fresh / Instacart autofill (US) (V). **Household:** none found (V-absence).
- **Price:** free with ads includes plans and lists; Pro removes ads and adds AI coach. Pricing page: $19.99/mo or $99.99/yr; App Store: $9.99/mo, $49.99/yr, "Max" $39.99/mo (conflicting). Web + iOS + Android (Android ~63k installs, 4.66; iOS 4.8, 1.1k). An Italian landing page exists (prospre.io/it); UI language unverified.
- **Fit:** cleanest "macro -> scaled meals -> list" engine; no exchange semantics, no household, US grocery integrations.

### 2.5 MealPrepPro (Nibble Apps)
- Batch-cooking planner: auto-plans the week to kcal/macro goals; recipes show macros per serving; recipes default to 1-2 servings so families scale manually (I). Vendor: "add a partner or family, set their goals separately, stay aligned on shared meals; the AI calculates portion sizes for your family" (V); sharing = second device logs into the same account (V help centre). Single consolidated list by aisle; no pantry tracking (V/I).
- **Complaints:** every recipe lands at similar kcal/protein ("scrambled eggs with bread came to almost 700 calories"); paywall resentment. **Price:** 7-day trial, $9.99/mo, $59.99/yr, $229.99 lifetime (App Store). iOS; Android contradictory between vendor pages. iOS 4.7 (10k).

### 2.6 PlateJoy (defunct, 1 Jul 2025)
- Was: household questionnaire; per-person portion tiers on shared recipes (S 1,050-1,350 kcal/day ... XL 2,100-2,700); pantry/fridge logging to avoid re-buying; aisle-sorted list; Instacart / Amazon Fresh; no custom macro ratios; $12.99/mo, $69/6mo, $99/yr (V/I). Worth copying: per-person portion tiers and pantry subtraction.

### 2.7 Mealime (defunct, 21 Oct 2026)
- Was: no calorie targets (Pro only filtered recipes by calories), servings only 2/4/6, aisle-sorted list that RESET whenever you edited the plan (the top complaint), Pro $2.99-5.99/mo (V). Lesson: list must survive plan edits (diff, keep checked state).

### 2.8 Lifesum
- Tracker; Premium "Programs & Meal Plans" are fixed-length curated templates (7 or 21 days, 3 meals + snack/day) with a digital shopping list for the week; not generated from your targets; list and planner disappear when the plan ends (V help centre). Entry conditions hide plans for some calorie goals (I). Price ~EUR 44.99-54.99/yr in EU storefronts, ~$12.99/mo (conflicting). Complaints: price, cancellation, "prescriptive not adaptive" (I). No household.

### 2.9 Yazio
- European tracker. PRO: pre-built weekly plans with recipes (3,000+) and grocery list (V). Complaint: list does not merge the same ingredient across recipes ("carrots in 2 recipes don't sum"), no supermarket-category ordering; custom macros and meal customisation were still feature requests (V user + support reply). Price ~EUR 6.99/mo, EUR 29.99-44.99/yr (conflicting). No household. Good metric / European food data (I).

### 2.10 Carb Manager
- Keto tracker. Premium: generate a plan from preferences and intolerances; "View macros per meal"; "Smart meal planner"; shopping list generator; option to "adjust the amount of food in your meal plan to help you meet your goals", with the honest caveat that sometimes it cannot scale and suggests foods to add (V help docs). $8.49/mo, $39.99/yr (US). 4.8 star. Keto-centric, no household.

### 2.11 MacroFactor
- Pure adaptive tracker: weekly check-in sets daily kcal/macro targets; no meal plans, no recipe library, no grocery list (V). $11.99/mo, $71.99/yr, no free tier. EU food DB weak (I). Irrelevant as a planner; relevant UX: weekly adjustment loop.

### 2.12 Cronometer
- Tracker, no planner (V). Per-meal view via diary groups (Gold can rename/number them); "Macro Scheduler" day templates; Nutrition Scores. Gold $10.99/mo, ~$49.99-59.99/yr. Irrelevant as planner.

### 2.13 Foodvisor (France)
- Photo-logging tracker. Premium: questionnaire -> personalised plan with daily kcal/macro recommendations and hundreds of recipes; no grocery list surfaced; reviews say recipes are generic and premium pressure is constant (V/I). ~EUR 9.99/mo or ~$83.99/yr (conflicting). Irrelevant as planner.

### 2.14 Nutrium (Portugal, dietitian SaaS)
- The closest formal model to G.'s diet: "lists of equivalents and food groups" (foods with similar macros that can be interchanged, e.g. a cereals group) shown to the client next to grams in the app/PDF; "suggest foods with equivalent quantities": the professional adds a main food and Nutrium computes the gram amount of each alternative matched on energy, fat, carb or protein (V help centre). Client app free; dietitian pays ~EUR 38 / 58 / 88 per month on annual billing (I). Not consumer, no generator, no Italian stores. Its equivalents data model is the one to copy.

### 2.15 Fitia (Latin America, 10M+ users claimed)
- Generates a weekly plan calibrated to kcal/macro targets (target shown as a +/-10% range); portions in grams or oz; recalculates portions across the day when you change one meal; grocery list uses personalised portions (vendor claims, V/I). **Household (V help centre):** "Sync Plan" links Premium accounts; the Master's meal structure is used for everyone; each person gets the same meals adapted to their own calories/macros; synced shopping list combines totals; Cooking Mode shows total ingredient quantities for all members. Family plan $89.99/yr for 2-6 users; individual $19.99/mo or $59.99/yr. Complaints: regenerating meals costs "coins"; generated meals can be bizarre (V store review). Best existing implementation of "same dish, different portions per person".

### 2.16 RP Diet Coach (Renaissance Periodization)
- Not a recipe generator but the closest to grammature logic: each meal has a target; you pick a protein source, carb source, fat source and veggies from macro-role lists and the app computes the grams of each to land within ~5 g per macro; lock foods and the rest recalculate; day-level rebalancing (V help/store). Rigid, dated UI; barcode scanner useless in Germany (reviews). ~$9.99/mo promo. Model to copy: meal = slots by macro role, pick a food per slot, grams are derived.

### 2.17 Italian apps and Italian-market tools
- **Melarossa** (consumer, IT): personalised diet with "sostituzioni integrate nella dieta" (swap dishes inside the plan, saved swaps) and shopping list with tick-off; website gives a printable diet and list free with ads; app PRO EUR 3.99/mo, 9.99/quarter, 34.99/yr after a 7-day trial (V vendor FAQ). Closest Italian consumer product, but the diet is Melarossa's own (not your dietitian's grammature), variety control is "remove up to two disliked foods", no price comparison.
- **Fitatu** (PL, Italian UI): 7 ready menus (Bilanciato, Keto, Vegetariano, ...), "Fitatu Diet" with substitutions and shopping list; Premium EUR 9.99-29.99 tiers (V store). Italian reviewer: ingredients "lontani dal mio modo di cucinare".
- **NutriCloud** (IT dietitian SaaS): "scambi isocalorici" with automatic quantity recalculation; patient web-app with plan, recipes, shopping list grouped by reparto with exact quantities; Start free up to 5 patients, Pro EUR 69/mo (V vendor). Professional only.
- **Dietosystem app / MetaDieta MyDiet / Nutribook**: patient apps tied to the dietitian's desktop software (plan delivery, diary, chat); no evidence of swap/equivalent features surfaced; quote-based pricing (V/I).
- **Smart Nutrition** (Dr. Petrini, web mini-app): substitutions "proteine con proteine, carboidrati con carboidrati, grassi con grassi, verdure con verdure" (V). Exactly the mental model, no planner.
- **Zone diet (blocchi):** Dieta a Zona (Nuova Eureka, iOS; pay-per-points, ~EUR 30 for a full diet, crash complaints); iZone Diet (iOS, free, 700 foods, Jan 2025 review asks for an automatic menu); ZoneIt (iOS + Android, free + Pro ~$1.99-3.99/mo, "Zonator" recipe block calculator added 19 Nov 2025) (V store). None generates a varied weekly plan plus list.
- **Diabetes exchanges:** no Italian app uses "liste di scambio"; Italian diabetes apps are carb-counting (Conta su di me, ContaTU, Dietometro, PhotoCarb, DID Plus). US "Exchange Diet" (iOS only) tracks the 8 ADA exchange groups with an exchange budget calculator (V store). Figwee is a photo-based portion-estimation diary, not exchange-based (V).
- **Canonical Italian equivalence values (dietitian blogs, V, consistent across 4 sources):** 80 g pasta secca ~ 110 g pane fresco ~ 80 g riso / orzo / farro / miglio / quinoa ~ 250-300 g patate crude ~ 180-200 g gnocchi ~ 100-110 g pasta fresca ~ 80 g farina di mais (~120 g polenta cotta); 50 g pane ~ 100-120 g patate. Protein-group equivalences (carne / pesce / uova / legumi / formaggi) are not published in gram form by those sources; take them from the dietitian's own sheet.
- **Italian supermarket price tools:** Spesami (lista + ricette con costo reale + confronto prezzi Esselunga / Coop / Conad / Lidl / Eurospin), Apprezzoo, RicordaPrezzi, IntelliList, PromoQui / DoveConviene (volantini). None covers Carrefour + Coop + Pam, none ties to a diet. Jow (FR, recipe-to-supermarket cart) is not available in Italy (V-absence).

## 3. The concrete gap

1. **Target semantics.** Every generator takes kcal/macro grams per day (ETM, Prospre, Strongr Fastr, Fitia) or per meal (Macrostax, RP, Carb Manager). None accepts the Italian prescription format "per pasto: 80 g fonte di carboidrati, 150 g fonte proteica, 10 g grassi, verdure a volonta" plus an equivalence table. Only Nutrium and NutriCloud (professional tools) model equivalents, and neither generates variety.
2. **Vegetables unlimited** is unrepresentable in macro optimisers (they count the carbs in vegetables and shrink the pasta to compensate).
3. **Variety is the weakest point of every generator** (ETM repetition by week 3-4; Strongr Fastr "same meals every day"; Fitia/MealPrepPro bizarre combos when the optimiser forces macros). A grammature diet makes this easier: quantities are fixed, so variety is a combinatorial choice over carb source x protein source x fat source x vegetables x cooking method x cuisine, not a numerical optimisation.
4. **Household.** Only Fitia (sync, per-person portions), ETM (people-count hacks) and the dead PlateJoy handled "same dish, different portions". None handles "one constrained person + one unconstrained person".
5. **Shopping.** Lists are per-recipe sums (Yazio does not even merge duplicates), rarely pantry-aware (PlateJoy did), never priced at Italian chains; store integrations are Instacart / Amazon Fresh (US) or Albertsons (US). No Italian tool ties a diet to Carrefour / Coop / Pam prices.
6. **Localisation.** ETM, Prospre, Strongr Fastr, Macrostax are English-first with US food databases; Yazio, Lifesum, Fitatu, Melarossa are localised but template-based.

## 4. Ideas worth borrowing (ranked)

1. **Slot model (RP Diet Coach + Nutrium).** A meal = slots by macro role (carb source, protein source, fat source, vegetables, optional extras). Each slot carries the prescribed grams and an equivalence list; a recipe is a template over slots; grams are derived, never optimised. Lock a slot and recompute the rest (RP); show the equivalents next to the grams (Nutrium).
2. **Fitia "Sync Plan" with a Master profile.** One meal structure for the household, per-person portions, cooking mode with total quantities, shopping list summing the household. Adapt: G. = constrained profile; partner = "same dish x1.3" or free profile.
3. **ETM's variety and economy controls.** Recurring foods (never / sometimes / often / always), "only use my foods", leftovers patterns (cook once, eat twice), "generator focus = groceries" (reuse ingredients to shrink the list), per-weekday targets.
4. **List hygiene.** PlateJoy pantry subtraction; Prospre date-range lists; Mealime's negative lesson (diff the list on plan edits, never reset, keep checked state); Yazio's negative lesson (merge identical ingredients and convert units).
5. **Variety as an explicit constraint.** Cooldowns per source (e.g. no protein source twice in 3 days), tags for cuisine and cooking method, a diversity score shown to the user; ETM proves a "variety slider" alone is not enough. Later: Prospre's "Fit Into Plan" to absorb a restaurant meal or treat and rebalance the day.

## 5. Recommendations for our app

- **Data model:** DietPrescription = meals -> slots (role, grams, allowed foods, equivalence table). Seed with the standard Italian carb equivalences above and let the dietitian's sheet override; keep macro values (CREA / USDA) only as an informational verification layer.
- **Generator:** combinatorial sampler with diversity constraints over slots, cuisines and methods; no macro optimisation; vegetables unconstrained.
- **Household:** Fitia-style profiles; partner multiplier on shared dishes; cooking view shows both portions and the total to cook.
- **Shopping:** weekly list = sum over slots x people, minus pantry, grouped by reparto; price layer per chain (Carrefour / Coop / Pam) as a separate module since no existing app or price tool covers that trio in Sanremo.
- **Do not compete with trackers** (MacroFactor, Cronometer, Yazio); offer export instead if ever needed.
